# Common photon-graviton light cone from one emergent metric

Status: **conditional infrared effective-theory theorem.**

Assume the candidate theory reaches an infrared phase with

1. one emergent Lorentzian metric `g_mu_nu`;
2. an unbroken massless metric spin-2 sector;
3. a deconfined compact `U(1)` sector whose leading local action is Maxwell;
4. both sectors couple to the same metric/Hodge geometry;
5. the vacuum is locally Lorentz invariant and no additional preferred tensor/foliation order parameter survives.

Then the leading photon and graviton propagation cones coincide, independently of the gravitational normalization `G` and electromagnetic stiffness `Z_A`.

---

## 1. Minimal coupled IR action

The leading parity-even local action is

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{IR}[g,A]
=\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[
\frac{R-2\Lambda}{16\pi G}
-\frac{Z_A}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
\right]+\Gamma_{irrelevant}.
}
\]

The two independent normalizations control response amplitudes/couplings.  They do not select two independent background metrics.

---

## 2. Photon principal symbol

Around a locally flat vacuum and after gauge reduction, the transverse Maxwell quadratic kernel is

\[
K_\gamma^{ab}(k)
=Z_A\,\delta^{ab}\,s+\cdots,
\qquad
s=-\omega^2+c^2\mathbf k^2.
\]

The positive constant `Z_A` multiplies the kernel but cancels from the pole equation.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{s=0}
\]

is the leading photon cone.

---

## 3. Graviton principal symbol

For the Einstein/Fierz-Pauli TT sector,

\[
K_g^{ab}(k)=Z_T\,\delta^{ab}\,s+\cdots
\]

with positive residue `Z_T` in the physical branch.  Hence the leading graviton pole is also

\[
\boxed{s=0.}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{c_\gamma=c_g=c_{metric}}
\]

in the common emergent metric units.

This equality is not obtained by fitting `G`, `Z_T` or `Z_A`; those coefficients multiply the kinetic operators rather than moving the leading characteristic cone.

---

## 4. Local higher-derivative form factors

Under the same Lorentz/gauge assumptions, the vacuum transverse photon kernel has the analytic form

\[
\boxed{
K_\gamma(s)=sF_\gamma(a_*^2s)
}
\]

with `F_gamma(0)=Z_A>0`.

Likewise the physical TT graviton kernel is

\[
\boxed{
K_g(s)=sF_g(a_*^2s),
}
\]

with `F_g(0)=Z_T>0`.

Therefore both original massless branches remain

\[
\boxed{s=0}
\]

at every finite order of a local analytic Lorentz-invariant derivative expansion.

Higher derivatives can modify off-shell response, interactions and introduce extra poles through zeros of `F`, but do not generate different perturbative vacuum light cones for the original two massless branches.

## 5. What can split the cones

A physical photon/graviton speed difference or vacuum dispersion requires additional structure, for example

- a preferred timelike/foliation field;
- a spatial orientational tensor condensate;
- different effective metrics generated by extra dynamical sectors;
- explicit/spontaneous Lorentz violation;
- nonlocal pole structure not reducible to functions of the common Lorentz scalar `s`;
- propagation on nontrivial backgrounds where curvature/matter creates sector-dependent effective characteristics.

Such structure must be derived from the microscopic history/state.  It cannot come solely from choosing a discrete regulator.

## 7. Relation to the fine-structure constant

The common light cone does not determine the electromagnetic coupling.

After canonical normalization of the compact phase field,

\[
e=Z_A^{-1/2},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac1{4\pi Z_A}
\]

in the repository's minimal-charge convention.

Therefore

```text
speed / causal cone       -> common metric/Hodge fixed point;
charge strength / alpha   -> independent phase stiffness ZA.
```

This prevents the measured speed of light from being used to tune the fine-structure constant or vice versa.

## 8. Blind tests

The coupled physical history calculation should preregister:

1. a common leading photon/graviton cone;
2. positive `Z_T` and `Z_A`;
3. no order-`k^2` anisotropy or polarization splitting in either sector in the Lorentz-universal branch;
4. no local analytic shifted vacuum massless pole if no preferred order parameter is found;
5. any observed nonzero splitting must be matched to an independently derived physical order parameter/background.

A failure of the common-cone test falsifies the minimal shared-metric infrared closure even if each sector separately looks healthy.
