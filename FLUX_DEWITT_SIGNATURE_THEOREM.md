## 1. Flux reconstruction

For a nondegenerate tetrahedron choose three independent oriented face-area vectors

\[
E_1=\frac12 b\times c,
\qquad
E_2=\frac12 c\times a,
\qquad
E_3=\frac12 a\times b.
\]

Define

\[
C=(2E_1,2E_2,2E_3).
\]

The edge matrix is reconstructed by

\[
A=\sqrt{|\det C|}\,C^{-T},
\]

and the spatial metric is

\[
\boxed{
q(E)=A^TA
=|\det C|\,C^{-1}C^{-T}.
}
\]

A common internal/spatial rotation `C -> R C`, `R in SO(3)`, leaves `q` invariant.  Thus the nine flux components contain exactly three rotational gauge directions and six metric directions.

## 2. DeWitt form

Use the standard ultralocal kinetic bilinear form, up to an overall positive normalization,

\[
Q_{DW}(\delta q)
=\operatorname{Tr}(q^{-1}\delta q\,q^{-1}\delta q)
-[\operatorname{Tr}(q^{-1}\delta q)]^2.
\]

On the six-dimensional vector space of symmetric metric variations it has one negative conformal direction and five positive traceless/shape directions.

Let `J=dq/dE` be the `6 x 9` Jacobian of the flux-to-metric map.  The pullback is

\[
\boxed{K_E=J^TG_{DW}J.}
\]

For every nondegenerate tetrahedral flux triple, the local map `E -> q` has rank six.  Its kernel is generated by common rotations

\[
\delta_\omega E_f=\omega\times E_f,
\qquad \omega\in\mathbb R^3.
\]

After quotienting these three directions, `J` is locally invertible.  By Sylvester's law of inertia the pullback has the same nonzero signature as the DeWitt form.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{inertia}(K_E)
=(5\text{ positive},1\text{ negative},3\text{ zero}).
}
\]

The three zeros are precisely Gauss/frame rotations, not manually removed modes.

## 4. Exact conformal flux direction

Scale all face fluxes uniformly,

\[
E_f\to(1+\epsilon)E_f.
\]

Then

\[
C\to(1+\epsilon)C,
\qquad
A\to(1+\epsilon)^{1/2}A,
\qquad
q\to(1+\epsilon)q.
\]

Hence `delta q=q` for unit infinitesimal scaling and

\[
\boxed{
Q_{DW}(q)
=\operatorname{Tr}I-[\operatorname{Tr}I]^2
=3-9=-6.
}
\]

Thus the unique negative direction has an immediate microscopic interpretation: **common radial scaling of the face fluxes**.
