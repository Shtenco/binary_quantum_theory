## 1. Motivation

The flat 4-simplex boundary algebra is

$$
\{H_k,H_l\}
=\frac{D_{lk}-D_{kl}}{3V}.
$$

At finite spin the volume operator has exact zero modes, so constructing a
literal `V^{-1}` introduces an unnecessary ordering/domain problem.  The
relational flux bridge gives a cleaner test.

Define

$$
Q_{kl}:=-i[H_k,H_l].
$$

The preferred Hermitian densitized quantum identity is

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{kl}^{dens}
:=
\frac32\{\hat V,Q_{kl}\}
-
\hbar(\hat D_{lk}-\hat D_{kl})
\longrightarrow0.
}
$$

In a semiclassical state where `V` and the bracket commute at leading order,

$$
\frac32\{V,Q\}\to3VQ,
$$

so this is precisely the inverse-volume-free version of the simplex HDA.

## 4. Scale-independent first gate

Overall Newton/time/operator normalization may differ between the finite model
and the classical convention.  Before fitting that one global normalization,
the first structural test is directional.

Let

$$
q=(q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4)
$$

be the four quantum dual responses and `r` the exact target vector above.
Define

$$
\boxed{
\Delta_{dir}
=
\sqrt{1-
\frac{|q^\dagger r|^2}{(q^\dagger q)(r^Tr)}}.
}
$$

A correct continuum principal direction requires

$$
\Delta_{dir}\to0.
$$

Only after the direction passes is one common scalar normalization allowed to
be estimated.  Independent rescaling of separate test functionals is
forbidden.

The null functional `F5` is checked separately and must remain compatible with
zero.

## 5. Why this is stronger than the old fixed-sector test

The old diagnostic asked how much of `[H_k,H_l]` returns to one fixed spin or
graph sector.  The densitized habitat identity instead evaluates the **full
graph-changing dual action** and compares its principal symbol with the actual
diffeomorphism vector generated by the canonical flux.

Therefore graph-changing components are neither automatically accepted nor
automatically called anomalous.  They pass only if their combined dual action
reconstructs the frozen nontrivial tangential structure function.
