# Lorentz representation theorem for an unshifted vacuum massless pole

Status: **symmetry statement stronger than the local derivative-expansion special case.**

The local theorem `K(s)=sF(s)` is useful for Wilson coefficients, but the basic no-dispersion conclusion follows from a simpler representation argument.

Assume an exactly Poincare/Lorentz-invariant vacuum and an unbroken massless gauge/diffeomorphism sector.  The physical transverse two-point pole condition cannot depend separately on `omega` and `|k|`; it can depend only on Lorentz invariants, for a single momentum principally

\[
s=k_\mu k^\mu=-\omega^2+c^2\mathbf k^2.
\]

A protected massless one-particle branch has its pole at

\[
\boxed{s=0.}
\]

Therefore a vacuum law of the form

\[
\omega^2=c^2k^2+\beta_4k^4+\beta_6k^6+\cdots
\]

with nonzero coefficients is not compatible with ordinary exact linear Lorentz invariance of that same massless branch, because it depends on the spatial momentum magnitude in a preferred decomposition.

This conclusion does not require the two-point form factor to be a finite polynomial.  Nonlocal Lorentz-invariant self-energies may contain logarithms, cuts and nonanalytic functions of `s`, but an unbroken massless pole remains a Lorentz-invariant pole at `s=0` unless the massless sector itself is altered.

Possible escapes require changed physical assumptions: Lorentz breaking/preferred order, a deformed/nonlinear realization of boosts, extra backgrounds/fields, mixing that changes the one-particle content, or propagation away from the Poincare vacuum.

Thus the project's quartic six-vector is best interpreted as a complete detector for possible preferred spatial structure at that order, not as a generic consequence of quantum gravity.
