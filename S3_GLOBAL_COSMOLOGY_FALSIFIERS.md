\[

## 2. Scalar harmonics are discrete

For a round three-sphere of radius `a`, scalar harmonics obey

\[
\boxed{
-\nabla^2Y_{n\ell m}
=\frac{n(n+2)}{a^2}Y_{n\ell m},
\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots
}
\]

with degeneracy

\[
\boxed{d_n=(n+1)^2.}
\]

Hence the physical global slice has a discrete infrared momentum spectrum rather than an exactly continuous `R3` spectrum.

At wavelengths much shorter than `a`, the level spacing becomes negligible and ordinary local Fourier momentum is recovered.

## 5. Physical continuum test

The finite PL completion proves the topology of the chosen recursive complex.  It does **not** by itself prove that the physical history measure selects that topology uniquely.

A global-topology physicalization must therefore test:

1. projector/history amplitudes for the `S3` sector under refinement;
2. competition, if allowed, with other topology sectors;
3. stability of the physical background saddle within the `S3` sector;
4. the relation of its curvature radius to the local coarse scale `a_*`;
5. global harmonic correlators against the local high-mode limit.

## 6. Falsification statement

If the theory ultimately claims that its canonical recursive `S3` is the actual global spatial topology, then a physical cosmological reconstruction requiring the opposite curvature/topology class would falsify that global claim even if the local GR sector remained correct.

Conversely, an observational value `Omega_k` extremely close to zero would not by itself falsify `S3`; the radius could simply be much larger than the Hubble scale.

The global topology claim must therefore be tested with global curvature/mode observables, not inferred from local flatness alone.
