# d_space=3 and compact U(1): dimensional compatibility with a massless photon phase

Status: **physics compatibility result, not a derivation that the microscopic phase is deconfined.**

The candidate construction independently produces

```text
q=2 -> selected spatial dimension d*=3
q=2 -> Hopf/Pancharatnam compact U(1) phase carrier.
```

These two outputs are nontrivially compatible with known compact gauge dynamics: pure compact U(1) in 2+1 spacetime dimensions has a monopole-driven confinement mechanism, while four-dimensional compact U(1) admits a nonconfining/Coulomb phase at sufficiently weak coupling.

Thus the selected `3+1` dimensionality is a dimension in which the q=2 compact phase carrier can in principle support a massless photon phase without adding noncompactness by hand.

---

## 1. The obstruction in 2+1 spacetime dimensions

For pure compact QED in `2+1` dimensions, monopole instantons generate confinement.  The classic result is A. M. Polyakov, *Quark confinement and topology of gauge theories*, Nuclear Physics B 120 (1977) 429-458, DOI `10.1016/0550-3213(77)90086-4`.

Therefore a bare compact U(1) phase carrier in two spatial dimensions would not generically imply a stable massless photon in the pure gauge sector.

Matter or other critical structure can alter that conclusion, so this is not a theorem about every 2+1-dimensional compact gauge system.

---

## 2. Four-dimensional compact U(1) admits a Coulomb phase

A. H. Guth, *Existence proof of a nonconfining phase in four-dimensional U(1) lattice gauge theory*, Physical Review D 21 (1980) 2291, DOI `10.1103/PhysRevD.21.2291`, gave a nonconfining weak-coupling result for four-dimensional compact U(1) lattice gauge theory.

Related duality/phase-structure analyses identify a four-dimensional regime resembling QED, with no electric confinement and a massless photon.

Therefore compactness itself does not forbid an emergent photon in `3+1` spacetime dimensions.

## 4. The remaining dynamical selector is the phase stiffness / RG basin

The Hopf bundle fixes compactness and topological charge normalization.  It does not fix the blocked Maxwell stiffness `Z_A`.

The microscopic phase-history calculation must determine whether RG flow gives

```text
confining/gapped compact phase
or
Coulomb/deconfined Maxwell phase.
```

Only the second branch supports the photon interpretation.

If the Coulomb branch is obtained, the same `Z_A` that characterizes the long-distance phase stiffness determines the canonically normalized electromagnetic coupling in the repository convention,

\[
\alpha=\frac1{4\pi Z_A}.
\]

No experimental value is used to choose the branch or tune `Z_A` before the microscopic result is frozen.

## 5. A new cross-sector falsifier

The selected dimension and phase dynamics now impose a joint requirement:

1. the q=2 geometric recursion must continue to approach three spatial dimensions;
2. the q=2 compact phase sector must flow to a positive deconfined Maxwell fixed point in that same geometry;
3. the photon and graviton must share the common leading metric cone in the Lorentz-universal branch.

A failure of item 2 means the q=2 phase fiber does not become physical light even if its gauge kinematics are exact.

Thus the theory is not allowed to infer electromagnetism from topology alone.
