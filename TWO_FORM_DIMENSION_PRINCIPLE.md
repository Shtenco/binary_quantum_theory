# Two-form dimension principle

Status: **conditional structural selector, not a proof that the frozen microscopic dynamics chooses four dimensions**.

## Starting point

The connection-first microscopic alphabet now contains two independently natural geometric objects:

1. an adjoint-valued face field `B^i`, obtained in the candidate blocking from one qubit on each oriented 2-cell;
2. the curvature `F^i(A)` of an edge connection, which is also a 2-form / 2-cochain.

Thus

\[
B^i\in\Omega^2,
\qquad
F^i(A)\in\Omega^2.
\]

No spacetime dimension has been used in these degree assignments: connection curvature is loop/2-cell data, and the face-qubit carrier is explicitly a 2-cell.

## Proposition — minimal metric-free local action selects degree four

Suppose the continuum action is required to be built locally from the **same microscopic fields**, without an externally supplied metric, Hodge star, volume density or an additional form field whose only role is to complete the degree.

The lowest-degree gauge-invariant pairing between the two natural sectors is

\[
\boxed{
B^i\wedge F^i(A),
}
\]

and the local Plebański metricity/simplicity invariant is

\[
\boxed{
B^i\wedge B^j.
}
\]

Both have form degree

\[
2+2=4.
\]

For such a local density to be integrated over the **whole emergent spacetime** as a top form with no extra structure, one requires

\[
\boxed{d=4}.
\]

Equivalently, in generic `d`-dimensional BF theory the field paired with curvature is a `(d-2)`-form.  Requiring that the `B` field live in the **same 2-cell microscopic alphabet** as curvature therefore gives

\[
d-2=2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{d=4}.
\]

## Why this is different from inserting a 4D lattice

The number four is not placed in a coordination number, tensor index range, lattice embedding or branch count.  It follows conditionally from three microscopic requirements:

\[
\boxed{
\text{edge connection}
\Rightarrow F\text{ on loops/2-cells},
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\text{binary quantum carrier is also on oriented 2-cells}
\Rightarrow B\in C^2,
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\text{local metric-free action uses no extra degree-completing field}.
}
\]

These conditions imply a four-form action density.

This is closely aligned with the Plebański formulation, where four-dimensional GR is an `SO(3)` BF theory supplemented by simplicity/metricity constraints on the `B` field.

## Relation to Hodge self-duality

The same dimension is independently special for the Hodge map:

\[
*:\Lambda^2\to\Lambda^{d-2}.
\]

For `d=4`,

\[
*:\Lambda^2\to\Lambda^2.
\]

Thus two conditional arguments coincide:

\[
\boxed{
B\wedge F\text{ is a top form}
\Longleftrightarrow
\text{2-form sector is Hodge self-dual in degree}
\Longrightarrow d=4,
}
\]

provided the stated microscopic assumptions are actually generated by the frozen rule.

## A stronger falsification requirement

This principle must **not** replace the dimension measurements in `BINARY_TO_GEOMETRY_GATE.md` and `MANIFOLD_DIMENSION_GATE.md`.

A serious frozen-rule result would need all of the following to agree in one scaling window:

\[
D_{\rm link}\to4
\]

for a full history/spacetime complex (or `3` on canonical spatial slices),

\[
d_s^{\rm history}\to4,
\]

\[
\Delta_{\rm Hodge}\to0,
\]

and a local coarse action dominated by the metric-free four-form sector

\[
\boxed{
S_{\rm eff}
=
\int
B^i\wedge F^i
+\text{simplicity terms}
+\text{irrelevant operators}.
}
\]

If independent topology/diffusion does not yield four dimensions, the form-degree argument is only a design choice and cannot be claimed as geometrogenesis.

## What would falsify the selector

The selector fails as an explanation of dimension if any of the following is necessary in the microscopic continuum limit:

1. an external metric or volume form before the geometric phase exists;
2. an additional fundamental `(d-4)`-form inserted solely to make `B wedge F` integrable in another dimension;
3. a dimension-dependent coordination rule or predefined 4-cell scaffold;
4. post-hoc projection onto a four-dimensional subcomplex;
5. independent graph/topology observables converging to a dimension other than four.
